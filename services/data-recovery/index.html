Data Recovery

If a drive is clicking, grinding, or failing, power it down now. Every extra minute of use can make recovery harder.

  • Supported Media: NVMe, SSDs, legacy HDDs, and corrupted RAID arrays.
  • Logical Recovery: Restoration of damaged partitions and file systems.
  • Physical Recovery: Extraction from drives with mechanical or firmware failures.
  • Strict Confidentiality: Media and recovered data are handled confidentially, copied only as far as the recovery requires, and returned or securely deleted when the job is done.

How It Works

You approve a quote before any recovery work starts.

  1. Contact us

    Use the contact form to describe the device, what happened, and which files matter most. Stop using the device in the meantime.

  2. Quote and drop-off

    We reply with any questions, an estimate, and local drop-off details.

  3. Evaluation

    We confirm whether the failure is logical (deleted files, corrupted file system) or physical (mechanical, electronic, or firmware), and confirm the price before going further.

  4. Recovery

    Where the drive allows it, we first make a full image and work from that copy, so the original is not stressed any further.

  5. Return

    You review the list of recovered files, and receive them on new media or a drive you provide. Your original device is returned to you.

Typical Timelines

These are typical ranges. Your quote gives the estimate for your specific device.

  • Evaluation: 1 to 2 business days after drop-off.
  • Logical recovery: usually 2 to 5 business days.
  • Physical or firmware recovery: usually 1 to 3 weeks, depending on whether donor parts are needed.
  • Urgent jobs: ask about priority handling when you get in touch.

Before You Drop Off

  • Power it down. Keep the device off. Repeated attempts to start a failing drive can cause more damage.
  • Skip DIY recovery tools. Recovery software can overwrite the very data you need, especially on a drive that is still in use.
  • Do not open the drive. Opening a hard drive outside a clean environment can make the platters unrecoverable.
  • List what matters. Note the folders, file types, or dates you need most so the recovery can focus on them first.
