Assessment Scope

An audit covers only the assets, environments, and time window named in the written scope. Engagements typically include one or more of the areas below. A third-party system is included only when the authorization names it.

Network

External and internal networks in the written scope: exposed services, separation between zones, and account paths between systems. Hosts that are not listed are not tested.

Web and Applications

Web applications, APIs, and custom software you designate. The review looks at authentication, authorization, session handling, and business logic that would affect confidentiality or integrity.

Wireless

Authorized review of wireless networks you control, including authentication, guest separation, and whether an unexpected access point on the named site is bridging into the internal network.

AI and ML Deployments

Assessment of AI and machine-learning systems you operate or are authorized to have tested: the model endpoints, the applications in front of them, the data they can reach, and the infrastructure they run on.

Engagement Method

Every engagement follows the same sequence, whether the scope is a single application or a broader environment. Testing does not start until authorization is in place.

  1. Scoping

    We agree the goal, the systems in scope, whether the work touches production, and what a useful result looks like. Systems that are out of scope are listed explicitly.

  2. Rules of engagement

    You provide written authorization from someone permitted to grant it. Together we set the testing window, emergency contacts, and any techniques or hosts that are excluded.

  3. Assessment

    Testing stays inside that agreement. The aim is to find and document weaknesses that matter, and to avoid needless disruption of the systems in scope.

  4. Reporting

    You receive a prioritized remediation report. Each finding names the affected asset, what was observed, the impact, a severity, and a recommended fix, so the most serious issues can be handled first.

  5. Retest

    After you apply fixes, we retest the findings we agreed to revisit and update each status. A retest checks those items. It is not a new assessment of everything else unless we scope one.

What You Receive

Two work products close the engagement: the report your team acts on, and a retest that shows what changed.

Prioritized Remediation Report

Findings ordered by severity. Each one includes the affected asset, what was observed, the impact, and a recommended fix. It is written so engineering can act and the people funding the work can see what to address first.

Retest

A follow-up pass on the findings you have remediated, with an updated status for each one: resolved, partially resolved, or still open. You keep that update with the original report.

Authorization and Confidentiality

  • Written authorization. We test only with written permission from someone allowed to grant it. A public website, a customer of yours, or a vendor platform is out of scope unless that authorization names it.
  • Rules of engagement. Scope, timing, points of contact, and excluded systems are agreed before testing. If a step would go past that agreement, we stop and ask.
  • Confidential handling. Findings, configuration details, and any client data encountered during the work are confidential. They are used to perform the engagement. We do not publish findings, client names, or samples from an engagement.
  • Access you provide. Credentials or access shared for a test are used only for that engagement and only inside the agreed scope. Reports go only to the contacts named in the agreement.
  • Point in time. An audit describes the agreed scope as it was during the test. It is not a promise that every weakness was found, or that the environment stays the same after later changes.
  • No secrets on the public form. Do not send passwords, keys, or sensitive data through the website. Share those only after an engagement is authorized, on a channel we agree in writing.
